P36|MARRIAGE REGISTRATION MODULE
P37|Introduction
P38|Marriage is “a religious sacrament in which a man and a woman are bound in a permanent relationship for the physical, social, spiritual, procreation and sexual needs; a formal union and social and legal contract between two individuals that unites their lives legally, economically, and emotionally”. The contractual marriage agreement usually implies that the couple has legal obligations to each other throughout their lives or until they divorce. Being married also gives legitimacy to sexual relations within the marriage.
P39|Overview
P40|Any citizen can use the service. This service generates a certificate that is accepted as legal proof of marriage. The SR is authorized to issue a Marriage certificate and solemnize the marriage. This service can be used at the SRO.
P41|Marriages are registered under the following acts:
P42|Hindu Marriage Act, 1955
P43|Special Marriage Act, 1954
P44|Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act, 1936
P45|Karnataka Marriage (Registration and Miscellaneous provision) Act, 1976
P46|Under the Hindu Marriage Act, you can register your marriage at the Registrar of Marriages in the jurisdiction where the marriage occurred or where the bride or groom resides. While the actual marriage rituals can be performed elsewhere, the filing or registration of the marriage is done at the SRO. In the case of a unique marriage, it is done through a notice of the intended marriage, and the SR solemnizes the marriage in his office.

P2314|Feedback from department dated 17/07/2026
P2315|Purpose
P2316|This document captures the department feedback received on 17/07/2026 concerning the Marriage Registration workflow in KAVERI 2.0, and translates each observation into a detailed requirement for the As-Is and To-Be process, the underlying rationale, and the business rules to be implemented. It covers four areas: Sakala initiation timing, mandatory Sub-Registrar (SR) declaration, Sakala completion timing, and appointment validity with the associated Sakala parking mechanism.
P2318|Sakala Initiation Timing
P2322|Description
P2323|Currently, the Sakala service-delivery timer starts the moment the SR clicks 'Generate Certificate', regardless of whether the bride, bridegroom, and witnesses are actually present at the counter.
P2324|In the offline Hindu Marriage, Intended Marriage, and Special Marriage flows, this means the SLA clock can run for the entire duration the application is pending — including time lost to no-shows or rescheduling — well before the DEO has even picked up the case. This creates an artificial SLA breach risk that does not reflect actual processing delay by the department.
P2325|The revised flow decouples the Sakala trigger from the 'Generate Certificate' click and ties it instead to the point of allocation to the DEO, which is the first concrete processing step after the SR has confirmed party presence. This aligns the SLA clock with genuine department-side processing time. Hindu Marriage Online is excluded from this change because, in that flow, certificate generation and digital signature happen in immediate succession with no DEO handoff in between, so the existing trigger already reflects real processing time.
P2326|From an implementation standpoint, this requires:
P2327|Moving the Sakala-initiation API call/event from the 'Generate Certificate' action to the 'Allocate to DEO' action, for the three affected marriage types only.
P2328|Ensuring the marriage-type check (Hindu Offline / Intended / Special vs. Hindu Online) is evaluated before deciding which trigger point to fire.
P2329|No new screen or field is needed — this is a backend trigger-point change to the existing SR workflow.
P2331|Business Rules
P2333|Mandatory Declaration by Sub-Registrar
P2336|Description
P2337|This requirement introduces a formal, system-enforced accountability checkpoint for the Sub-Registrar. Today, an SR can generate a certificate, allocate it to the DEO, or digitally sign it without any explicit, recorded confirmation that the bride, bridegroom, and witness details have actually been reviewed. The new mandatory declaration closes this gap by requiring an affirmative action — a checkbox tied to a fixed declaration statement — before the SR can proceed to the next irreversible step (digital signature or DEO allocation).
P2338|The placement of the checkbox differs by marriage type because the point of 'no return' differs: for Hindu Marriage Online, that point is the digital signature, so the declaration gates the 'Digital Sign' button. For the offline/Intended/Special Marriage flows, the first irreversible handoff is allocation to the DEO, so the declaration gates the 'Allocate to DEO' button instead. In both cases, the governing UI rule is the same — the next-step button is disabled by default and only enables once the declaration checkbox is ticked.
P2339|Implementation considerations:
P2340|Add a non-editable declaration label/text with an adjacent checkbox control on the 'Generate Certificate' screen (or the immediately following screen) for both flows.
P2341|Digital Sign button (Hindu Marriage Online) and Allocate to DEO button (other three flows) must be disabled by default and enabled only on checkbox selection — this should be a client-side state check reinforced by a server-side validation to prevent bypass via direct API calls.
P2342|The checkbox state and timestamp should ideally be logged for audit purposes, since this is a compliance/accountability control.
P2343|Business Rules
P2345|Completion of Sakala Application
P2348|Description
P2349|Marking Sakala as complete at the DEO's 'Ready for Issue' click currently serves an operational purpose — it keeps the DEO queue from accumulating unresolved items — but it misrepresents the actual point of service delivery. The digital signature by the SR is the legally and procedurally definitive act that completes the marriage registration; certificate issuance by the DEO afterward is an administrative follow-up, not the substantive service delivery event.
P2350|The revised requirement moves the Sakala completion marker to coincide with the SR's digital signature. Consequently, for Intended Marriage and other marriage types where a post-signature DEO issuance step still exists, that step must be explicitly excluded from Sakala time computation — otherwise completion time and the SLA clock could fall out of sync, or the system might inadvertently double-count or continue counting after the true point of completion.
P2351|Implementation considerations:
P2352|Move the Sakala-completion event/flag from the DEO 'Ready for Issue' action to the SR digital-signature action.
P2353|Explicitly exclude the DEO issuance step (post SR signature) from any Sakala timer/SLA calculation logic for Intended Marriage and other marriage types.
P2354|Confirm with the department whether the existing DEO-queue-management benefit (applications not stacking up) needs a separate operational control now that it is decoupled from Sakala completion.
P2356|Business Rules
P2358|Appointment Validity
P2361|Description
P2362|Today's flow has no expiry on a scheduled appointment: an application can sit in the SR login well past the appointment date with no system-driven consequence, whether or not the parties ever showed up. This creates stale, indefinitely pending applications in the SR queue and offers no incentive for citizens to appear on the scheduled date, since a missed appointment carries no penalty and does not release the slot.
P2363|The to-be requirement introduces a hard 24-hour validity window tied to the scheduled appointment date. If the parties do not appear within that window, the system shall automatically return the application to the citizen rather than leaving it pending in the SR queue. Critically, such returned applications are treated as terminated, not paused — the citizen must restart with a fresh application, rather than resuming from the stage they left off. This is a significant procedural requirement and should be clearly communicated to citizens at the time of appointment scheduling, since it affects fees already paid and documents already submitted.
P2364|The department has additionally flagged a 'Sakala Parking' mechanism as a note for further discussion: instead of immediately returning a non-appearance case to the citizen, the application would first move to a parked state. This is presented as a note to the department rather than a finalized requirement, because the current KAVERI 2.0 / Sakala framework only supports two states — Initiated and Completed — with no intermediate 'parked' or 'paused' state. Introducing parking would require defining: how long an application may remain parked, whether the Sakala SLA clock pauses or continues while parked, what triggers a transition out of parking (resumption vs. auto-return to citizen), and whether a parked application still counts toward pending-application MIS metrics.
P2365|Implementation considerations:
P2366|Introduce a 24-hour validity timer keyed to the scheduled appointment date/time for the SR-login stage of offline Hindu Marriage, Intended Marriage, and Special Marriage applications.
P2367|On expiry without party appearance, auto-trigger return-to-citizen and mark the application as non-resumable, requiring fresh submission.
P2368|Sakala Parking should remain an open item requiring explicit sign-off from the department on state definition, SLA-clock behavior, and MIS/reporting treatment before it is built, since it is currently outside the Initiate/Complete model.
