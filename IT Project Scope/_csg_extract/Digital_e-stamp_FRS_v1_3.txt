P41|Kaveri 2.0
P42|The Department of Stamps and Registration (hereinafter referred to as the “Department”) is entrusted with the custody, maintenance, and preservation of registered public records, including but not limited to the registration of marriages and firms, documents evidencing transactions such as sale, transfer, or gift of immovable properties, mortgages, powers of attorney, wills, and other instruments of legal or commercial significance. The Department, in addition to its custodial functions, is responsible for the levy and collection of stamp duty on behalf of the Government for transactions and instruments as prescribed under law. These services are administered through 258 Sub-Registrar Offices (SROs), operating under the supervision of 35 District Registrar Offices across the State.
P43|Within this framework of Kaveri 2.0, optionally registrable articles occupy a distinct position under the Karnataka Stamp Act. These are instruments on which stamp duty is payable, though their registration under the Registration Act, 1908 is not compulsory. Such articles include documents relating to immovable property, movable property instruments, wills, powers of attorney, adoption deeds, bonds, affidavits, and diverse commercial agreements. While the payment of stamp duty is essential to validate such documents, parties retain the discretion to seek or forego registration, weighing the benefits of creating a public record and securing evidentiary value against the additional formalities of registration.
P44|Context of digital e-Stamp
P45|Currently, stamp duty for optionally registerable articles in Karnataka is collected primarily offline through the sale of non-judicial stamp paper and franking at authorised centres. Parties can purchase physical stamp paper from licensed stamp vendors, cooperative banks, treasury offices, or sub-registrar offices by paying the required denomination in cash or online. Alternatively, documents may be franked using mechanical impression machines at registration offices or designated franking centres, where duty is paid in cash/online and a numbered frank is affixed to the instrument. Receipts are issued on payment, and parties manually affix or incorporate the stamped or franked papers into their documentation for legal validation.
P46|This document describes the functional features of the digital e-Stamp in Kaveri 2.0. Citizens can now apply for and obtain a digital e-Stamp for optionally registrable documents through Kaveri 2.0. It calculates stamp duty based on the property's value, type, and location, as provided by the user, and allows payments via UPI, net banking, or cards. Upon payment, a unique e-Stamp certificate is generated instantly, and citizens can download the digitally signed e-Stamp document from the dashboard. This ensures faster processing and transparency. The system streamlines workflows while reducing dependency on physical stamp vendors.

P164|Fee calculations
P165|30. The fee for an e-Stamp article shall be calculated based on the existing logic for document registration, specifically mapped to pre-defined e-Stamp articles. The calculation logic for each article is determined by one of four parameters:
P166|Market Valuation: 
The fee is based on the current market value of the asset or property to which the document pertains.
P167|Consideration Value:
The fee is calculated based on the consideration (the amount agreed to be paid or transferred), as specified in the document.
P168|Fixed Stamp Duty:
The fee is a fixed value, predefined for the article, regardless of market or consideration value.
P169|Highest of All:
The fee is determined as the highest value among the applicable calculated fees (such as consideration)
